The appliance leaks that do the most damage

Supply lines cause the worst appliance leaks because they are under constant pressure. A braided washer hose that bursts will spray until someone closes the valve. Dishwashers and ice makers are slower but can run for days behind cabinetry before anyone sees a stain.

Drain-side failures behave differently. A clogged washer standpipe or a dishwasher drain hose that slips off dumps grey water, which is a different category from clean supply water. That distinction affects what can be dried in place.

Stopping an appliance leak before it spreads

1

Close the valve

Turn the small shutoff behind or beside the appliance. Closing the main works if you cannot reach it.

2

Unplug if safe

Unplug only when your hands and the floor are dry. Otherwise switch off the breaker.

3

Contain the water

Use towels or a wet vacuum on the surface, then call before anything is moved off the floor.

Mahopac notes

Appliance leaks in lake-area homes

Laundry rooms in the Mahopac area often sit on the lower level or off the garage, and the finished space beside or below them is where the water ends up. A washer valve that fails in a basement laundry area wets carpet, stored items and sometimes the sump pit.

The Town of Carmel building department regulates permitted work, so if repairs involve plumbing or structural changes, ask them what applies. We handle the water damage portion and can note what we find for your plumber.

What to do before we arrive: appliance leak

  • Shut off the supply valve behind the appliance, and unplug it only if the cord and floor are dry.
  • Do not touch the appliance if you are standing in water.
  • Move small items off the floor and lift area rugs.
  • Keep the failed hose or part, and photograph the label and the damage.

Reference standard

We use the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard as a reference for how wet structures are evaluated and dried. That is a statement about the standard we follow, not a claim of any certification. Where a leak came from a dishwasher or washing machine, we also look at the cabinet base and subfloor.

Documenting an appliance loss

  • Photograph the appliance, its model plate and the water line before cleanup.
  • Note the age and whether it was recently serviced or moved.
  • Keep the failed part, such as the hose, since the insurer may want to see it.
  • Save receipts for emergency cleanup and the appliance repair.

Appliance leaks are often covered when sudden. A slow hidden leak may fall under wear and tear. Your policy governs, so talk to your agent.
