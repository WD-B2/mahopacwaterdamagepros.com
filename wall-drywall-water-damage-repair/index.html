How water travels inside a wall

Drywall is gypsum between paper faces. Water enters through the paper and gypsum softens, and the board wicks water upward like a wick. A puddle that reaches the baseboard can leave drywall wet a foot or two above the floor.

Inside the wall, water follows studs, plates and wiring holes. Insulation holds it against the framing, and a wall that feels dry on the surface can be wet behind. Moisture readings from the surface and a thermal camera show what is happening.

How a wall repair proceeds

1

Locate the wet area

Meters and a thermal camera map the wicking line and the hidden cavity.

2

Cut and open

Drywall is cut above the wet line, often a flood cut at a consistent height, so the cavity can dry.

3

Dry the framing

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry studs and plates.

4

Replace and finish

New insulation, drywall, tape, mud, texture and paint complete the repair.

Mahopac notes

Plaster, drywall and older walls in the Mahopac area

Older homes in the Mahopac area can have plaster and lath in some rooms and drywall in others. Plaster dries more slowly and is more likely to crumble when soaked, so replacement of plaster sections may be needed even after a clean water leak.

Homes near the lakes may also have exterior walls that have taken water through siding and window trim over time. If a stain keeps coming back after a repair, a contractor should look at the exterior as well. We handle the interior drying and finishing.

Insulation and electrical in a wet wall

Fiberglass batts that are saturated lose their insulating value and hold water against the framing, so wet batts are normally removed and replaced after the cavity dries. Spray foam and rigid board behave differently and are evaluated case by case.

Wall outlets, switches and wiring that were soaked should be checked by a licensed electrician before they are used again. Water in a wall can reach the box even when the room looks fine, and the circuit can be left off until the wall is dry.

Wall damage and insurance

A sudden leak that wets a wall is often covered. A stain that has been growing for months may be treated differently. Photograph the wall before it is opened, keep the damaged drywall if the adjuster asks to see it and save every receipt. Coverage is between you and your insurer.

Reference standard

We use the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard as a reference for how wet structures are evaluated and dried. That is a statement about the standard we follow, not a claim of any certification. Wall cavities are checked with readings before a decision is made on removal.

Before you patch it yourself

  • Confirm the leak is fixed and the wall is dry with a moisture reading.
  • Do not paint over wet drywall or a stain with ordinary paint.
  • Check for wet insulation behind the board by looking through an outlet cover.
  • Take photos before opening the wall.

For ceiling damage, see ceiling water damage repair. If the wall was wet by a plumbing failure, burst pipe water damage covers the cleanup from the source.
