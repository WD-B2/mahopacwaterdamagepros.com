Jefferson Valley notes

How Tropical Storm Isaias showed the town's weak points

The Town of Yorktown hazard plan, which covers Jefferson Valley, records Tropical Storm Isaias on August 4, 2020. The storm brought down 58 trees and caused power outages that lasted up to seven days in places. Seven days without power means seven days of sump pumps that do not run and refrigerators that thaw and leak.

A long outage is where a battery backup for a sump pump is worth the price. Around-the-clock dispatch is available for the town regardless of the weather.

Where Yorktown records localized flooding

Jefferson Valley is a census-designated place that sits mostly in the northeast corner of Yorktown, with US 6 running through it. The Taconic State Parkway is on the western side of the town and the Putnam County line is to the north. The plan names localized flooding in the Hunterbrook and Greenwood Street areas, along Brookside Avenue, on Hunterbrook Road near Fox Tail Lane, on Mohegan Avenue and Dale Street and on New Road in Shrub Oak.

Those streets are not all in Jefferson Valley, but they sit in the same town and drain into the same systems. If you live near any of them, water that rises on the road can reach a lower level or a crawl space.

Permits and who answers for Jefferson Valley

Repairs that need permits in Jefferson Valley go through the Town of Yorktown Building Department at 363 Underhill Avenue, which also handles floodplain regulations. The town's flood law requires new construction in mapped flood zones to be built two feet above the base flood elevation.

Jefferson Valley sits in Westchester County, a different county from Mahopac, so county health and highway authorities differ from the ones that serve our home base. The Jefferson Valley Mall and the retail along US 6 are commercial properties where commercial water damage restoration may apply.
