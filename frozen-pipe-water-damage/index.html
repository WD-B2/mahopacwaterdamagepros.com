Why the damage comes after the thaw

A frozen pipe is blocked with ice, and the ice itself holds the split closed. The pressure between the ice plug and a closed faucet is what ruptures the wall of the pipe. When temperatures rise or the heat comes back on, the ice melts, and a pipe that was already cracked suddenly has an open path.

That is why people leave for work with a dripping faucet and come home to a ceiling on the floor. If you suspect a pipe is frozen, close the main shutoff before thawing anything. Water that cannot enter the house cannot leak out of a split.

If you think a pipe is frozen right now

1

Shut the main

Close the main valve first so a hidden split cannot release water when it thaws.

2

Open the faucet

Open the nearest tap on the frozen line so steam and water have somewhere to go.

3

Warm gently

Use warm air from a hair dryer or space heater at a safe distance, never an open flame or torch.

If water has already appeared, skip the thaw and call. A plumber repairs the split, and we remove the water and dry what it reached. Our water extraction page explains the pumping step.

Mahopac notes

Cold-weather plumbing in the Mahopac lake belt

Mahopac grew into a year-round community, but a lot of its housing started as lake cottages, and older additions, enclosed porches and garages can have plumbing that was never designed for deep cold. That mix of old and new construction is where freeze splits tend to hide. A line run through an exterior wall to a bathroom addition is a classic example.

The Town of Somers hazard plan, covering Baldwin Place, notes icing along Route 6, which gives a sense of how hard freezing conditions hit this corridor. We do not claim a frequency for local freezes, but if your home sits near the lakes or on open ground along the Route 6 corridor, wind exposure on exterior walls matters.

What drives the cost after a frozen pipe splits

Where the split was

A split in an open basement is a mop-up. A split behind a finished wall means opening it, drying it and rebuilding it.

How long it ran

A leak caught in minutes versus one that ran over a weekend changes the drying area.

Insulation and drywall

Wet insulation usually has to go. Drywall is judged by readings and any contamination.

Flooring below

Water that dropped to a lower level adds rooms and materials.

Equipment days

Dense framing and closed cavities take longer to dry than open spaces.

Repair scope

Drying is one line. Replacing finishes is another that you can schedule separately.

We scope drying and repair separately in writing so you can compare the numbers with your claim.

Documentation your insurer will want

  • Photos of the split pipe, the ice and the water damage before cleanup.
  • The date and approximate time you found the leak and when the heat was last on.
  • Receipts for emergency plumbing, extraction and drying.
  • A list of damaged contents with approximate ages and values.
  • Moisture readings from the drying job, which we provide.

Frozen pipe losses are commonly covered when the house was heated and the damage was sudden. Policies differ, so read yours and ask your agent about any condition on heat or vacancy.

What to do before we arrive: frozen or split pipe

  • Shut off the main water valve before you try to thaw anything.
  • Open the faucet on the frozen line, and warm the pipe gently with a hair dryer, never an open flame.
  • Check for splits at the frozen section before turning the water back on.
  • Photograph any wet walls and ceilings below the pipe.

Reference standard

Preventing the second freeze

After repair, it is worth asking a plumber about insulating exposed lines, sealing air leaks around pipe penetrations and relocating a vulnerable run. Leaving a faucet at a slow drip during a hard freeze keeps water moving, and opening cabinet doors lets room heat reach supply lines.
