Why basements flood and what that means for cleanup

Basement water comes in through foundation cracks, floor and wall joints, window wells, floor drains, failed sump pumps and sewer backups. Each source carries a different category of water, and that decides how the cleanup goes.

A basement is also the lowest part of the house, so water that arrives upstairs usually ends up there. A burst pipe on the second floor, a washer failure or a storm that overwhelms the sump all flow downhill to the same space.

Mahopac notes

Basement flooding around Lake Mahopac

Mahopac's lakes, streams and sloped lots put many basements near the water table. The Putnam County hazard plan records that Hurricane Sandy blew out culverts in the Town of Carmel and flooded basements, and it names the Lake Secor outlet stream as a source of flooding when vegetation chokes it.

The July 9, 2023 storms damaged roads, bridges and culverts across Putnam County. Even if your street stayed open, a high water table can push water through foundation joints. A sump pump is only as reliable as its power and its float switch.

Cleaning up a flooded basement

1

Check safety

Power, gas and the furnace are checked first. We do not enter water that is live.

2

Pump and extract

Water is pumped out in stages so the foundation walls are not stressed by sudden pressure change.

3

Remove and discard

Wet carpet, pad, drywall and stored items that cannot be cleaned are removed.

4

Clean and dry

Surfaces are cleaned, and dehumidifiers dry the concrete and framing.

Pumping in stages matters. Emptying a basement too fast while the ground outside is saturated can push the walls inward. We pump in steps and watch the water outside.

Electrical and furnace safety

Basements hold the panel, the furnace and often the water heater. Do not enter a basement with standing water if any of them might be live. Power can be cut from the main breaker if it is dry and reachable, and the utility can disconnect service if it is not. Have equipment inspected before you restart it.

Waterproofing is not restoration

We pump, clean, dry and rebuild after water arrives. We do not waterproof foundations or install drainage, and a basement that floods every spring needs that work. If you are in that situation, ask a waterproofing contractor and a plumber about grading, drainage and the sump system, and let us clean up what water has already done.

Reference standard

We use the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard as a reference for how wet structures are evaluated and dried. That is a statement about the standard we follow, not a claim of any certification. In a basement that matters because concrete and framing dry at different rates.
