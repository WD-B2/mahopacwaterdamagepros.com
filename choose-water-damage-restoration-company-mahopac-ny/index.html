How to Choose a Water Damage Restoration Company in Mahopac

You may have minutes to choose someone. These questions, from licensing to the scope in writing, help you compare companies calmly even when water is on the floor.

Why the choice matters more than it looks

Water damage restoration is not just pumping and fans. A job that ends too early leaves wet framing, and the cost shows up later as odor, paint failure and floors that move. A job that tears out too much costs more than it should. The difference comes down to measurement, scope and honesty.

You are also hiring a company to enter your home, handle your belongings and talk to your insurer. Reliability and clear paperwork matter as much as equipment.

Ten questions to ask any company

  • Are you licensed and insured, and can I see proof of insurance?
  • Will you take moisture readings and give me a copy of the log?
  • Do you follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard?
  • Will you put the scope and cost factors in writing before work expands?
  • Will you explain what you are removing and why?
  • Can you document the loss with photos for my insurer?
  • Who will actually be at my house, and are they employees?
  • How do you handle contaminated water and disposal?
  • What do you do if drying readings do not drop as planned?
  • Will you coordinate with my plumber, roofer or adjuster?

Mahopac notes

After a countywide event, expect busy companies

Putnam County declared a state of emergency on July 10, 2023, after severe storms damaged roads, bridges and culverts. After an event like that, out-of-area crews often arrive and go door to door. Some are fine, and some are not accountable if something goes wrong. Check how long the company has a local presence and whether it has a real address.

Ask the Town of Carmel building department what registrations or permits apply to the work you plan. A company with a local base, such as ours on Route 6 in Mahopac, is easier to reach if a question comes up after the job. We serve Mahopac Falls and Putnam Valley as well.

How the first call should go

A good company asks questions before it quotes. It wants to know where the water came from, how long ago, how much is on the floor, whether the water is still running and whether anyone is in danger. It should tell you what to do now, such as shutting off the main or turning off breakers, before it talks about price.

Be wary of a call that opens with a sales pitch or a request for a credit card. You should leave the call knowing what happens next, who is coming and what information they need.

Reading the authorization before you sign

An authorization lets the company begin work. Read what it says about scope, payment, insurance assignment and who owns the documentation. Some forms assign your insurance benefits to the contractor, which gives them control over the claim. You may not want that.

Ask for a copy, ask what the cost factors are and ask whether you can approve drying and repair separately. If a form is rushed in front of you with no time to read, treat that as a warning.

Checking references and proof

Ask for the business address, proof of insurance and two or three references from recent jobs. Call the references and ask whether the scope matched the final bill, whether the company kept appointments and how the crew treated the home. Look for reviews on sites you trust and read the negative ones as carefully as the positive ones.

Do not rely on a logo or a truck. Ask who will be at your house and whether they are employees or subcontractors, because accountability depends on knowing who did the work.

What a fair scope looks like

A good written scope lists the rooms affected, the methods used, the equipment placed and a plan for monitoring. It separates emergency work, drying and repair. It says what is excluded, such as plumbing repair or waterproofing, so there are no surprises.

Compare scopes, not totals. Two companies can give different numbers because one includes demolition and the other does not. Our pages on structural drying and flood damage restoration describe how we think about scope.
