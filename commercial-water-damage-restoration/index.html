Why commercial water damage is different

A business loss involves more people. There is a property owner, one or more tenants, an insurer for each and often a lease that decides who pays for what. The building itself is also more complex, with suspended ceilings, shared walls, commercial carpet tile and HVAC above the ceiling.

Time matters differently too. A retail space that cannot open loses revenue every day, and a restaurant has health inspection implications. We plan the work around keeping the unaffected parts of the building operating where it is safe to do so.

How we run a commercial job

1

Secure the building

Water source off, power isolated where wet, area barricaded and occupants moved.

2

Extract and protect

Standing water goes out first and inventory and equipment are moved or covered.

3

Dry the structure

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry walls, floors and ceilings with readings logged.

4

Repair in phases

Repairs are staged so parts of the space can reopen as they are finished.

Mahopac notes

Commercial districts around Mahopac

Mahopac's commercial core runs along Route 6, around Clark Place and South Lake Boulevard, with marinas along the southern shore of Lake Mahopac. The Putnam County hazard plan names the Route 6 and Clark Place area among flood-prone locations in the Town of Carmel, which makes it a place where storm water and business interruption overlap.

Older buildings along these corridors can have additions, basements under storefronts and wiring that has changed over decades. If your property is leased, check who is responsible for the building shell and who covers tenant improvements before the adjuster arrives.

What drives commercial restoration cost

Square footage

Larger affected areas multiply equipment and labor.

Building systems

Suspended ceilings, shared walls and HVAC add steps.

Contents

Inventory, records and equipment have to be moved or documented.

After-hours work

Overnight and weekend work keeps businesses open but takes crews off other jobs.

Phasing

Staged repairs add visits but reduce downtime.

Tenant versus owner

Who pays for which part of the building is set by the lease and insurance.

We scope the building, the contents and the drying in writing and break out what is drying and what is repair.

Documentation for commercial claims

  • Photos and video of the loss before cleanup.
  • A list of damaged inventory, equipment and records.
  • Moisture readings and drying logs from our job.
  • Dates and times of each response and the work performed.
  • Your lease and policy, which decide who files and who pays.

We cannot state your policy coverage, but a business insurance policy often has separate sections for property and lost income. Ask your agent which applies.

What to do before we arrive: commercial loss

  • Stop the source and secure the area so staff and customers stay out of wet zones.
  • Protect inventory, records and equipment, and photograph them first.
  • Tell your landlord, property manager or insurer as your lease and policy require.
  • Make a list of affected rooms and the hours each was wet.

Reference standard

We use the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard as a reference for how wet structures are evaluated and dried. That is a statement about the standard we follow, not a claim of any certification. Commercial drying plans are documented with readings so building owners and insurers can review them.
