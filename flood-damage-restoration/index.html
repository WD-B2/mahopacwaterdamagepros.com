Why flood water is a different problem from a pipe leak

A burst pipe delivers clean water. Flood water arrives from storm runoff, overflowing streams and lakes or a backed-up sewer, and it carries soil, bacteria and chemicals with it. That puts it in the contaminated category, and it changes what can be dried in place and what has to be thrown away.

The materials matter too. Porous items like carpet, pad, insulation and fiberboard usually cannot be cleaned after flood contact. Drywall that wicked contaminated water often has to be cut out above the water line. Solid wood framing and concrete can be cleaned and dried, which is why we stage the work from removal to cleaning to drying.

What the local flood record says

The Putnam County hazard mitigation plan rates flood risk in the Town of Carmel as low overall, with pockets of real trouble. It lists flood-prone locations including Lake Shore Drive, the Route 6 and Clark Place area, Hill Street at Route 6, Memory Lane and Sandy Street. It also records that vegetation in the Lake Secor outlet stream has caused flooding and road closures.

The plan's insurance data shows how rarely flood policies exist compared with the exposure. As of July 28, 2020, the Town of Carmel had 73 National Flood Insurance Program policies, with 122 claims paid since the program began and 10 repetitive-loss properties. Mahopac's lakes mean groundwater can also rise on properties that never see a stream.

The Town of Carmel adopted its Flood Damage Prevention Law on January 9, 2013, and requires elevation of new construction in mapped flood zones to two feet above the base flood elevation. Ask the town building department how those rules affect repairs to a substantially damaged structure.

How we handle a flood cleanup

1

Secure and assess

Power, gas and structural safety are checked first. We photograph water lines and note the source.

2

Pump and remove

Water goes out first, followed by affected carpet, pad, insulation and contaminated drywall.

3

Clean and dry

Surfaces are cleaned with antimicrobial products appropriate to the water category, then dried with air movers and dehumidifiers.

4

Rebuild

New drywall, insulation, flooring and finishes go in once readings confirm the structure is dry.

Flood water categories in plain terms

CategoryWhere it comes fromHow we handle it
Category 1Broken supply line or clean rain intrusionExtract and dry in place when caught early
Category 2Washer or dishwasher discharge, sump overflowClean, treat and dry, often replacing porous materials
Category 3Flood water, sewage, long-standing waterRemove porous materials, clean thoroughly, dry framing

Flood water is normally treated as Category 3. Water left standing can move from one category to the next.

What decides the price of flood restoration

Depth and duration

Water that stood for days soaks deeper into framing than water that was pumped out quickly.

Contamination

Silt, sewage and runoff add removal, cleaning and disposal steps.

Affected area

Finished basements and ground-floor living areas cost more to rebuild than unfinished spaces.

Materials lost

Carpet, pad, insulation and drywall that cannot be cleaned are replaced rather than dried.

Drying length

Structural framing and concrete dry slowly and keep equipment on site longer.

Rebuild choices

Flooring grades, trim and paint are your decisions and follow your budget.

Scope is written and explained before repair work begins, with drying and rebuilding priced as separate parts.

Flood insurance and what a standard policy leaves out

The New York Department of Financial Services notes that a standard homeowners policy generally does not cover flood. Flood damage is normally insured through a separate policy such as one backed by the National Flood Insurance Program. Water that backs up through drains or a sump pump may be covered by an endorsement, but often not when a flood caused the backup.

Whatever your coverage, document the damage before cleaning, keep damaged items until the insurer says otherwise and save every receipt. We provide photos, readings and written scope for the claim file.

What to do before we arrive: flood

  • Stay out of standing water until power to the area is confirmed off.
  • Do not walk into a flooded basement, and do not use electrical appliances in wet rooms.
  • Treat floodwater as contaminated and keep children and pets out.
  • Photograph the high-water line and damaged items before moving them.

Reference standard

We use the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard as a reference for how wet structures are evaluated and dried. That is a statement about the standard we follow, not a claim of any certification. Floodwater is generally treated as contaminated under that standard, which affects what porous materials can be saved.
