Mahopac notes

Storms that have hit Putnam County

The Putnam County hazard mitigation plan lists several storms that affected the Town of Carmel. These include Hurricane Sandy, the October 29 and 30, 2017 nor'easter and nor'easters on March 2 and March 7, 2018. A tornado came on May 15, 2018, and a storm on February 7, 2020. An April 13, 2020 wind and rain event had gusts around 50 miles per hour. Sandy blew out culverts and flooded basements.

More recently, severe storms on July 9, 2023 damaged roads, bridges and culverts across the county, and the County Executive declared a state of emergency on July 10. Storms like these do not only flood basements. They tear shingles, break windows and loosen siding, and water finds the gaps.

How storm water gets in

Wind-driven rain is the quiet one. It pushes water sideways under siding, past window flashing and through roof edges, and the leak shows up days later as a stain. Roof damage lets water in from above and it collects in the attic first.

Ground water is the loud one. Saturated soil and overwhelmed drains push water through foundation cracks, window wells and floor drains. A power outage from the same storm can stop the sump pump when it is needed most, which is why a battery backup is worth asking a plumber about.

What to do before we arrive: storm damage

  • Stay clear of downed lines and flooded basements until power is confirmed off.
  • Cover openings with tarps only if you can do so safely.
  • Move belongings to dry rooms and photograph the damage.
  • Call your insurer and keep receipts for emergency repairs.

Reference standard

We use the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard as a reference for how wet structures are evaluated and dried. That is a statement about the standard we follow, not a claim of any certification. Storm water can carry soil and debris, which we account for in the cleaning plan.

Storm claim documentation

  • Photograph the damage and the weather conditions as soon as it is safe.
  • Record the date and time of the storm and when you found the leak.
  • Keep receipts for tarping, boarding and emergency drying.
  • Keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them.
  • Ask your insurer separately about wind, water backup and flood, since they are often handled differently.

The New York Department of Financial Services advises homeowners to file claims as soon as possible and to make only the repairs needed to prevent further damage until the insurer has inspected.
