Storm Season Home Prep for Mahopac, NY Water Damage

Putnam County has had enough storms in recent years to write a checklist from them. Here is what to do before the next one to keep water out, protect your sump pump and leave yourself a good record.

Mahopac notes

The storms Putnam County has actually had

The Putnam County hazard mitigation plan lists events that affected the Town of Carmel, including Hurricane Sandy. It also lists a nor'easter on October 29 and 30, 2017 and nor'easters on March 2 and March 7, 2018. A tornado came on May 15, 2018, and a storm on February 7, 2020. A wind and rain event on April 13, 2020 had gusts near 50 miles per hour. Sandy blew out culverts and flooded basements.

More recently, severe storms on July 9, 2023 led the County Executive to declare a state of emergency on July 10. Roads, bridges and culverts were damaged across the county. The lesson is not that any single storm is predictable. It is that water events in this area come from many types of weather in different seasons.

Outside the house

  • Clean gutters and downspouts and extend downspouts several feet from the foundation.
  • Check that the ground slopes away from the house on all sides.
  • Clear leaves from window wells and make sure drains are open.
  • Look at the roof from the ground for missing shingles and failed flashing.
  • Trim branches that hang over the roof or rub against it.

Inside the house

  • Test the sump pump by pouring water into the pit and watching it run.
  • Ask a plumber about a battery backup, since outages often come with storms.
  • Know where the main water shutoff is and label it.
  • Move valuables off the basement floor and into plastic bins.
  • Photograph the house and contents now, so you have a record of how things looked.

Power and sump pump safety

Storms often knock out power exactly when the sump pump is busiest. A battery backup pump or a water-powered backup, installed by a plumber, keeps the pit from overflowing. If you use a generator, keep it outdoors and well away from windows, and have an electrician install a transfer switch rather than plugging it into a wall outlet.

Also check the discharge line. A frozen or blocked line sends the water right back into the pit, so the pump runs and the pit fills anyway.

A one-page storm record

Write down your insurer's claim phone number, your policy number, the location of the main shutoff and the name of a plumber and a roofer. Put it with a flashlight and a battery radio. In the hour after a storm, you will not want to search for these.

Know your roof, your basement and your neighbors

Walk the property after the first heavy storm of the season and look for water stains, pooling and soft ground. Check the attic with a flashlight for dark patches on the underside of the roof deck, which show a slow roof leak. Look at the basement walls for new damp lines.

Neighbors are part of the plan too. A clogged culvert or a stream that backs up affects several properties, and a call to the town highway department can help. Exchange phone numbers with the nearest neighbors so you can warn each other when water starts to rise.

Mistakes homeowners make before a storm

  • Testing the sump pump only when the pit is already full.
  • Leaving downspouts that discharge beside the foundation.
  • Storing important papers and photos on the basement floor.
  • Assuming the homeowners policy covers flood.
  • Waiting until the storm has started to look for the main shutoff.

What to do during and right after a storm

Stay away from downed lines and flooded roads. If water begins to enter, shut off power at the panel only if it is dry and safe to reach. Move to higher ground if a lower level floods quickly.

Afterward, photograph the damage before moving anything. If a roof is open, call a roofer for a temporary cover.
