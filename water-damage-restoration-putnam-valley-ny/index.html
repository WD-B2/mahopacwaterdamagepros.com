The Town of Lakes and what it means for water damage

Putnam Valley is a neighboring town in southwestern Putnam County, and its hazard plan describes it as the Town of Lakes. Water is part of nearly every property decision, from septic systems to lake levels. The Carmel plan lists Putnam Valley as a bordering community.

Around-the-clock emergency dispatch is available across the town, including the hamlet of Lake Peekskill, which has its own ZIP code, 10537, and the main Putnam Valley ZIP, 10579.

Putnam Valley notes

Oscawana Lake, silt and septic systems

The Putnam Valley hazard plan names silt flooding from Oscawana Lake as a problem that affects roads and homes, especially along Dunderberg Road and Coleman's Landing Road. It also says the Oscawana dam in Abley Park lacks level control, which floods properties and septic systems when the lake rises.

A flooded septic system is different from a flooded basement. The plan says septic systems in the town are regulated through the Putnam County Department of Health, and a lake or dam overflow can push water and effluent back toward homes. If sewage has backed up, see sewage backup cleanup.

Flood insurance and building rules in the town

As of July 31, 2014, the plan counted 77 National Flood Insurance Program policies in force in Putnam Valley, with 62 claims paid totaling $1.4 million since 1978. Five properties were repetitive-loss properties and two were severe repetitive-loss. Flood insurance maps were updated on March 4, 2013.

The town's Flood Damage Prevention law is in Chapter 136 and requires one- and two-family homes to be built two feet above the base flood elevation. The plan lists about two dozen roads as flood-prone. If your road is on that list, your basement or crawl space may share the problem. Ask the town building department how the rules apply to repairs.
