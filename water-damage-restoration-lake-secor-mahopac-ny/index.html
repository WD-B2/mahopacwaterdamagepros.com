Where Lake Secor sits

Lake Secor is one of several lakes inside the Mahopac hamlet, along with Lake Mahopac, Kirk Lake, Lake Casse, Teakettle Spout Lake and Long Pond. Mahopac is a hamlet in the Town of Carmel, so the building department, the town flood law and the hazard plan that apply to a home near the lake are the Town of Carmel's. Mail goes to ZIP 10541.

We do not claim a boundary for the Lake Secor neighborhood, because we found no official one. Local real estate sites use the name for homes around the lake, and the county hazard plan speaks of lakeshore properties. If you are unsure whether we cover your street, call and give us the address.

Lake Secor notes

What the Putnam County hazard plan says about Lake Secor

The Town of Carmel section of the Putnam County hazard mitigation plan says that Lake Secor historically floods because of vegetation overgrowth within and along its outlet stream. It adds that the flooding closes adjacent roads and affects lakeshore properties. The plan also lists a proposed project to dredge the outlet stream, with a cost estimate of $250,000. We have not verified whether that work has been carried out.

For a homeowner, the useful point is the cause. When an outlet is choked, water backs up in the lake and spreads to low ground nearby, and that is a flood loss rather than a plumbing loss. Flood water is treated as contaminated, and flood damage is commonly handled differently by insurers, so photograph the water line and read your policy before you start repairs.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. The FEMA Flood Map Service Center will show the mapped zone for a specific address.

Before the crew arrives at a lake-area home

Stop at any step that is not safe. These steps apply to most water losses.

Look before you walk

Stay out of deep water, and do not wade through water that may reach outlets, cords or a basement panel.

Close the main

Turn off the water supply only if you can reach the valve without stepping in water.

Keep clear of dirty water

Treat lake, stream and drain water as contaminated, and keep children and pets away.

Lift what you can

Move papers, electronics and small items up and out if the room is safe to enter.

Photograph the damage

Take photos of the water line, the source and damaged belongings before cleanup starts.

Documenting a loss for your insurer

Whether a loss is covered depends on the cause and on your policy, and we cannot tell you what an insurer will pay. A standard homeowners policy and a flood policy are separate products, so ask your agent which applies to rising water and which applies to a pipe failure.

What we can do is document the work. Photos, moisture readings and a written scope show what was wet, what was removed and how the structure was dried, and you can give those records to your insurer.
