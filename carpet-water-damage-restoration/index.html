When wet carpet can be saved

The water source decides the outcome. Clean water from a supply line that was caught quickly often allows the carpet to be extracted and dried in place, and the pad is often replaced separately. Grey water from a washer or dishwasher is more borderline. Sewage or flood water means carpet and pad come out.

Time matters too. Carpet that stays wet for days is harder to save, and odors set in. The sooner water is extracted, the better the odds.

How carpet drying works

1

Extract water

Weighted wands pull water from carpet and pad.

2

Lift and ventilate

Carpet is lifted from the tack strip at the edge so air moves under it and the pad is removed if needed.

3

Dry the floor

Air movers dry the underside and the subfloor. The dehumidifier controls the room.

4

Reinstall or replace

Carpet is stretched and reset after the floor is dry, or new carpet and pad go in.

Mahopac notes

Carpeted lower levels in Mahopac homes

Finished basements with wall-to-wall carpet are where we see the most carpet loss. The Putnam County hazard plan records that Hurricane Sandy flooded basements in the Town of Carmel through blown-out culverts, and sump failures during storms add to the list. Carpet in a lower level that floods usually does not survive contaminated water.

If the water came from a storm or a sewer backup, see storm water damage cleanup or sewage backup cleanup, since those change the carpet decision.

Carpet over concrete and carpet over wood

Carpet over a concrete slab dries differently from carpet over a wood subfloor. Concrete holds water and releases it slowly, so a dehumidifier does the heavy lifting and the pad may need to come out entirely. A wood subfloor can absorb water and swell, and it has to be dried before new pad goes down.

Area rugs are a separate case. They can usually be cleaned and dried off site, and the floor underneath gets its own drying.

Odors after wet carpet

A musty smell means moisture is still present somewhere, usually in the pad, the tack strip or the subfloor. Deodorizing without drying only masks it. We dry first, then treat what remains, and we tell you if the pad or subfloor has to be replaced before the smell goes away.

Reference standard

We use the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard as a reference for how wet structures are evaluated and dried. That is a statement about the standard we follow, not a claim of any certification. The standard is also the reason carpet and pad are judged by water type and how long they stayed wet.

What to do with wet carpet

  • Remove furniture and put aluminum foil or blocks under the legs of furniture you must leave.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on wet carpet.
  • Keep fans running and the room ventilated if the water was clean.
  • Take photos of the carpet and note the water source.
