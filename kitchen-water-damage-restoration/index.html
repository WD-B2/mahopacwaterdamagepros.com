Why cabinets make kitchen leaks worse

Particleboard and MDF cabinet boxes soak up water like a sponge and swell permanently. Solid wood and plywood handle moisture better, but finishes can lift and joints can separate. A leak that runs for even a day can ruin the bottom of a base cabinet.

Water also runs under cabinets, where it cannot be wiped away. Toe kicks and the space beneath them need to be opened to dry, and the floor beneath the cabinet run may be wet even when the visible floor looks fine.

Mahopac notes

Older kitchens and lake-house plumbing

Many Mahopac homes have kitchens that have been remodeled around older plumbing. Copper lines, galvanized remnants and shutoff valves from different decades sit behind cabinets that were installed later, and the weak link is often a valve that has not been turned in years.

If your remodel involves permits, the Town of Carmel building department handles them. We do not claim that any particular fixture is common in the area. We only note that old valves and supply lines are worth replacing when you open up a wall or cabinet.

Appliances, countertops and what stays in place

Moving a refrigerator or dishwasher is part of finding the water. Both can hide a slow leak, and the floor under them stays wet long after the visible floor dries. We pull appliances only when needed, protect the floor as we go and put them back when drying is complete.

Countertops usually stay unless water is trapped beneath them. Stone and laminate tops are bonded to cabinet boxes, so removing them to dry a cabinet is a decision that depends on readings and on how well the cabinet can be dried in place.

Kitchen water damage and your insurance policy

A sudden failure such as a burst supply line or an overflowing dishwasher is often covered under a homeowners policy, while a leak that went on for weeks may be treated as wear and tear. Keep the failed part, photograph the cabinet interior and keep receipts. Your agent can tell you how your policy treats each case.

What to do before we arrive: kitchen leak

  • Close the shutoff under the sink and turn off the dishwasher.
  • Empty the cabinet base and set items on a dry surface.
  • Remove standing water with towels and avoid running a household vacuum on it.
  • Photograph the cabinet interior, the floor and the supply lines.

Reference standard

We use the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard as a reference for how wet structures are evaluated and dried. That is a statement about the standard we follow, not a claim of any certification. Cabinet bases and toe kicks hold moisture, so readings are taken there before repairs start.
